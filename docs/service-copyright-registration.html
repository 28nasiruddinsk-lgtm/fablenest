How Book Copyright Registration Works

Copyright registration is an official record that you are the author of a book. In many countries copyright exists automatically, but registering gives you a public record that can help if someone copies your work. This page is information, not legal advice.

Rules differ by country, including what can be registered, the fees and the forms. That is why we first confirm which office applies to you.

We help with the administrative steps: gathering details, completing the application and submitting it. For legal questions about your rights, please speak with a qualified lawyer.

Who this is for

  • Authors who want an official record of authorship
  • Authors preparing to publish
  • Authors who are not sure which forms apply to them

Key terms, in plain words

Copyright
The legal right of the author to control how a work is copied and shared.
Registration
An official public record of a work and its author.
Filing fee
The fee paid to the copyright office when the application is submitted.

Questions Authors Ask

Quick, honest answers about copyright registration. Can’t see yours? Send us a message and we’ll reply.

Ask a question
Is my book protected by copyright without registering?

In many countries copyright exists automatically when you create the work. Registration adds an official public record, which can be useful if you ever need to prove or enforce your rights.

What do I need to register a book?

The title, your name as author, details of publication and a copy of the work. We tell you exactly what is needed for your situation.

Is this legal advice?

No. We help with the administrative process only. For legal questions about your rights, please speak with a qualified lawyer.

Which country’s office do you register with?

It depends on where you live and where you publish. We confirm the right office with you before filing.
