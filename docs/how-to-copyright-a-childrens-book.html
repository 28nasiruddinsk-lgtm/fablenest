Do I Need to Copyright My Children’s Book?

In most countries your children’s book is protected by copyright automatically, the moment you write it down or draw it. You do not have to register to own the copyright. Registration is an optional official record that makes it much easier to prove your ownership and, in some countries, to take legal action.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. For a specific dispute or contract, speak to a lawyer in your country.

What Copyright Protects (and What It Does Not)

  • It protects the words of your story and the illustrations as they are written and drawn.
  • It does not protect ideas, plots or themes, such as "a lost bear finds his way home".
  • It does not protect book titles or short phrases; those can sometimes be protected as trademarks instead.
  • It does not protect a general character type (a talking fox), but a specific, distinctive character drawn in a certain way is protected as artwork.

How to Register a Children’s Book in the United States

  • Decide what you are registering: the text, the illustrations, or the finished book as one work. If you wrote and illustrated it yourself, you can register both together.
  • Create an account with the US Copyright Office online registration system.
  • Choose the right form of application. A single author who owns the work usually qualifies for the lower-fee option.
  • Fill in the title, the author’s name, the year of completion and whether the book has been published.
  • Upload a copy of the work (or send one by mail if you cannot upload it) and pay the fee.
  • Wait for the certificate. Processing takes weeks to months; the effective date is the day your application is received.

Who Owns the Illustrations If I Hire an Illustrator?

If you hire an illustrator, the pictures belong to the illustrator by default unless a written agreement says otherwise. This is the most common copyright problem in children’s publishing.

Before work starts, agree in writing whether you are buying the artwork outright (an assignment of copyright) or a licence to use it in your book, and for which formats, countries and years. The agreement should also say whether the illustrator may reuse the characters.

We provide a clear written agreement for every illustration project, so ownership and usage rights are settled before any artwork begins.

Common Copyright Myths

  • "Mailing myself the manuscript is enough." It is weak evidence and does not replace registration.
  • "The © symbol is required." It is not; it is useful but optional.
  • "An ISBN protects my book." An ISBN identifies an edition for sellers; it gives no copyright protection.
  • "Publishing on Amazon KDP transfers my rights." It does not; you keep the copyright and give a licence to sell.

Children’s Book Questions, Answered

Quick, honest answers about how to copyright a children’s book. Can’t see yours? Send us a message and we’ll reply.

Ask a question
How do I copyright a children’s book?

Your book is protected automatically once you write it down or draw it. In the United States you can add an official registration through the US Copyright Office; in the UK and Australia there is no registration system, so you keep dated drafts as evidence.

How much does it cost to copyright a children’s book?

Copyright itself is free and automatic. If you register in the United States, the online fee is in the tens of dollars for a single author and one work; check the Copyright Office fee page for the current amount.

Do I have to register to own the copyright?

No. Registration is optional in most countries. In the United States it is required before you can file an infringement lawsuit, which is why many authors register soon after finishing the book.

Who owns the illustrations in my picture book?

The illustrator does, unless a written agreement transfers the copyright or licenses it to you. Agree on this in writing before any artwork is made.

Can I copyright a book title?

No. Titles are not protected by copyright, though a title used as the name of a series may qualify for trademark protection.

Is the copyright symbol required?

No. The © symbol with your name and the year is not required, but it is good practice because it tells readers who to contact.
